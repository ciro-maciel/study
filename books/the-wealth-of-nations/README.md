**In one sentence:** a people's wealth comes not from the gold it hoards but from the work it produces, and work produces more when it is divided among specialists, which only happens when the market is large enough to exchange what each one makes.

## The five main ideas

**1. The division of labour multiplies production** (Book I, chapter 1). The pin factory: ten people, each doing one part, together made over 48 thousand pins a day; working alone, perhaps not twenty each.

> "Those ten persons, therefore, could make among them upwards of forty-eight thousand pins in a day."

**2. For three reasons, and the third is machines** (Book I, chapter 1): the dexterity of those who repeat, the time not lost switching tasks, and

> "the invention of a great number of machines which facilitate and abridge labour, and enable one man to do the work of many."

**3. The division of labour is limited by the extent of the market** (Book I, chapter 3):

> "When the market is very small, no person can have any encouragement to dedicate himself entirely to one employment, for want of the power to exchange all that surplus part of the produce of his own labour..."

Nobody specializes without someone to sell to. The larger the market, the finer the division can be, and the richer society becomes.

**4. Exchange coordinates without anyone in command** (Book I, chapter 2, and Book IV, chapter 2):

> "It is not from the benevolence of the butcher, the brewer, or the baker that we expect our dinner, but from their regard to their own interest."

> "...led by an invisible hand to promote an end which was no part of his intention."

**5. The bad side, in Smith's own words** (Book V, chapter 1). The same division that enriches can dull whoever performs the same operation all their life:

> "The man whose whole life is spent in performing a few simple operations [...] generally becomes as stupid and ignorant as it is possible for a human creature to become."

That is why he argued for public education for working people. The book remembered as praise of the market itself carries the price of the leap.
