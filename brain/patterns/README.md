# Patterns

[Português](README.pt-BR.md) · [Brain](../README.md)

What the [leaps](../README.md#the-leaps-so-far) have in common. Each pattern says in which leaps it appears and where it fails; a pattern with no exception has not been tested enough. These seven are the first reading, made on 31 October 2026 with six leaps and one book.

## 1. A cost collapses

Every leap makes cheap something that was expensive: connecting computers ([internet](../leaps/internet.md)), publishing ([web](../leaps/web.md)), reaching people ([social networks](../leaps/social-networks.md)), producing text and reasoning ([AI](../leaps/ai.md)), repetitive physical work ([robots](../leaps/robots.md)). It does not get a little cheaper: it gets so cheap that people who would never have used it start using it.

**Question for the frontier:** what is still expensive and could become almost free?

## 2. The idea comes decades early

| Idea | When | Became real | Gap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Memex](../leaps/memex.md), trails between documents | 1945 | Mosaic browser, 1993 | 48 years |
| the word "robot" | 1921 | Unimate, 1961 | 40 years |
| Licklider, the computer as a communication medium | 1960 | TCP/IP on ARPANET, 1983 | 23 years |
| Transformer | 2017 | ChatGPT, 2022 | 5 years |

The gap is shrinking. The idea for the next leap is probably already written, in a paper, a book or an essay, waiting for a cost to fall.

**Question for the frontier:** which old idea is only waiting for a cost to fall?

## 3. An open, common layer

The internet won with TCP/IP, which nobody needed permission to use. The web won because CERN released the code royalty-free, on 30 April 1993. AI grew on published papers and, now, open protocols for connecting models to tools.

**The exception:** [social networks](../leaps/social-networks.md). The middle layer is closed: the profile and the reach belong to the company. It was the leap that concentrated the most power and the one most in need of correction today. The exception reinforces the pattern: when the layer is closed, the leap happens, but the bad side grows.

## 4. The leap happens at the interface

The technology exists years before it becomes a leap. The leap is the day an ordinary person can use it: the browser for the internet, the feed for connections, conversation for AI. For robots, the interface has not arrived, and it may be the same as AI's: saying in words what you want.

## 5. Every new participant is worth something to all

On the internet, the web and social networks, each new machine, page or person makes the network more useful to those already in it. That is why these leaps accelerate past a certain point. In AI the effect shows differently: the more people use it, the more is learned about what works.

## 6. The bad side arrives with it

No leap arrived with only its good side: the internet without security, the web with no filter for the false, social networks with captured attention, AI that is confidently wrong, the robot that displaces work. And in every case the bad side was dealt with afterwards. [Adam Smith](../books/the-wealth-of-nations/README.md) was already writing, in 1776, about the price the division of labour charges those who live it.

**Question for the frontier:** can the next leap be designed with its bad side dealt with from the start?

## 7. The market grows, and work divides further

From [The Wealth of Nations](../books/the-wealth-of-nations/README.md), Book I, chapter 3: the division of labour is limited by the extent of the market. Each leap enlarged the market for something and so allowed specializations that could not sustain themselves before: the niche specialist the web connected to readers across the world, the creator who lives off their own audience, the person who coordinates agents.

**Question for the frontier:** which market is still too small to sustain those who would specialize in it?
