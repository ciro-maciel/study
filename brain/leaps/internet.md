# Internet (1969 to 1983)

[Português](internet.pt-BR.md) · [Brain](../README.md) · Previous: [Memex](memex.md) · Next: [web](web.md)

**In one sentence:** the internet made it cheap to connect distant computers, from different makers, into one network, with a common, open rule for them to talk.

## What existed before

Computers were rare, expensive and isolated. Each network belonged to one maker or one institution and did not talk to the others. In 1960, J. C. R. Licklider was already writing about "man-computer symbiosis", and in 1968, with Robert Taylor, about "the computer as a communication device". The idea existed; the network was missing.

## The leap

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| 29 Oct 1969 | First message on ARPANET, the network funded by the US Department of Defense, between UCLA and SRI |
| 1974 | Vint Cerf and Robert Kahn publish the protocol that becomes TCP/IP: a rule for connecting **different networks** to each other |
| 1 Jan 1983 | ARPANET switches to TCP/IP in production. The "network of networks" is born |

## The analysis

- **What became cheap:** sharing expensive machines and exchanging data between distant places.
- **The open layer:** TCP/IP. Anyone can implement it, nobody has to ask permission, and any network that follows the rule can join.
- **The interface for people:** almost none, for a long time. For two decades the internet belonged to researchers and the military; it reached ordinary people only with the [web](web.md).
- **What it connected:** machines, and through them the first groups of people (email and mailing lists).
- **The bad side:** the network was designed among people who trusted each other. Security was not in the design, and much of today's trouble (viruses, attacks, spam) comes from that.
